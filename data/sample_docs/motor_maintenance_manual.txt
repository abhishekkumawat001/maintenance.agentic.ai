ELECTRIC MOTOR MAINTENANCE MANUAL
==================================

1. INTRODUCTION
---------------
Electric motors are the workhorses of industrial operations, converting electrical energy into mechanical energy. They account for approximately 70% of industrial electricity consumption. Proper maintenance extends motor life (typically 15-20 years), prevents unplanned downtime, and maintains energy efficiency.

2. MOTOR TYPES AND APPLICATIONS
---------------------------------
- AC Induction Motors (Squirrel Cage): Most common, used for pumps, fans, compressors
- AC Synchronous Motors: Used where precise speed control is required
- DC Motors: Variable speed applications, older installations
- Variable Frequency Drive (VFD) Motors: Modern speed control, energy savings

3. ROUTINE INSPECTION SCHEDULE
-------------------------------

Daily:
- Check motor running temperature (frame temperature should not exceed nameplate rating + 40°C ambient)
- Listen for unusual sounds (grinding, humming, buzzing)
- Check for unusual vibration
- Verify motor amperage is within nameplate Full Load Amps (FLA)
- Inspect for signs of overheating (discoloration, odor)

Monthly:
- Measure and record vibration levels (overall velocity: acceptable < 4.5 mm/s per ISO 10816)
- Check bearing temperatures with infrared thermometer
- Inspect motor cooling fins and ventilation openings for blockage
- Verify all connections are tight (terminal box, grounding)
- Check VFD parameters and fault logs if applicable

Quarterly:
- Perform insulation resistance test (megger test)
  * Minimum acceptable: 1 MΩ per kV of rated voltage + 1 MΩ
  * Example: 460V motor = minimum 1.46 MΩ (round to 2 MΩ)
  * PI (Polarization Index) should be > 2.0
- Full vibration spectrum analysis with trending
- Thermographic survey of motor, connections, and cables
- Check alignment with driven equipment

Annual:
- Comprehensive insulation testing (megger, PI, surge comparison)
- Bearing lubrication or replacement
- Clean motor interior (compressed air, max 30 PSI)
- Check air gap uniformity (for larger motors)
- Inspect brushes and commutator/slip rings (DC and wound rotor motors)
- Verify motor grounding resistance
- Update motor condition assessment record

4. BEARING MAINTENANCE
-----------------------

Grease-Lubricated Bearings:
- Re-grease interval depends on speed, size, and operating conditions
- General rule: Relubrication interval (hours) = 14,000 - 70 × d (bearing bore in mm)
- Use manufacturer-recommended grease type (do NOT mix grease types)
- Remove relief plug before greasing to prevent over-pressurization
- Add grease with motor running if safe to do so
- Quantity: Volume (oz) = D × W / 10 (D=OD, W=width in inches)

Oil-Lubricated Bearings:
- Check oil level weekly
- Oil change interval: 6-12 months or per oil analysis results
- Use ISO VG 68 mineral oil for most applications (check manufacturer specs)
- Monitor oil temperature (typically 40-60°C above ambient)
- Perform oil analysis quarterly for large/critical motors

Signs of Bearing Failure:
- Increasing vibration levels, especially at bearing defect frequencies
- Rising bearing temperature
- Unusual noise (growling, whining, clicking)
- Grease discoloration or hardening
- Shaft play (axial or radial)

5. INSULATION SYSTEM
---------------------

Insulation Classes and Temperature Limits:
- Class A: 105°C maximum
- Class B: 130°C maximum  
- Class F: 155°C maximum (most common in modern motors)
- Class H: 180°C maximum

For every 10°C above rated temperature, insulation life is halved.

Insulation Testing:
- Megger Test: Apply DC voltage and measure insulation resistance
  * Test voltage: 500V for motors up to 1kV, 1000V for motors 1-2.5kV
  * Record ambient temperature and correct readings to 40°C
- Polarization Index: Ratio of 10-minute to 1-minute megger reading
  * PI > 4.0: Excellent
  * PI 2.0-4.0: Good
  * PI 1.0-2.0: Questionable — investigate
  * PI < 1.0: Poor — motor should be removed from service
- Surge Test: Detects turn-to-turn insulation weakness

6. COMMON FAILURE MODES
-------------------------

Overheating:
- Causes: Overload, voltage imbalance, blocked ventilation, high ambient temperature
- Prevention: Monitor amperage, ensure proper ventilation, check voltage balance (< 1%)

Bearing Failure:
- Causes: Over/under lubrication, contamination, misalignment, shaft currents (VFD induced)
- Prevention: Follow lubrication schedule, use shaft grounding rings for VFD applications

Winding Failure:
- Causes: Insulation degradation, moisture ingress, voltage spikes, thermal cycling
- Prevention: Regular megger testing, proper motor enclosure, surge protection

Shaft/Coupling Issues:
- Causes: Misalignment, coupling wear, key/keyway damage
- Prevention: Laser alignment after installation and periodically, coupling inspection

7. ENERGY EFFICIENCY
---------------------
- Oversized motors operate at low efficiency — right-size replacements
- Replace older standard motors with premium efficiency (IE3/IE4) models
- Use VFDs for variable load applications (potential 20-50% energy savings)
- Maintain power quality: voltage within ±10%, current balance within 1%
- Clean motor regularly to maintain cooling efficiency

8. SAFETY
----------
- LOTO procedures mandatory before any electrical or mechanical work
- Verify zero energy state with voltage tester before touching terminals
- Use insulated tools rated for the voltage class
- Ground motor frame and conduit properly
- Maintain safe clearances from rotating parts
- Arc flash analysis required for work on motor control centers

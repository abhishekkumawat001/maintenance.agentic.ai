CENTRIFUGAL PUMP MAINTENANCE GUIDE
===================================

1. OVERVIEW
-----------
Centrifugal pumps are the most common type of pump used in industrial applications. They work by converting rotational kinetic energy from a motor into hydrodynamic energy of fluid flow. Proper maintenance is critical to prevent catastrophic failures and ensure optimal performance.

2. ROUTINE MAINTENANCE SCHEDULE
--------------------------------

Daily Checks:
- Check suction and discharge pressure gauges
- Monitor pump vibration levels (acceptable: < 4.5 mm/s)
- Check bearing temperature (should not exceed 82°C / 180°F)
- Listen for unusual noises (cavitation, bearing noise)
- Verify proper seal operation (no excessive leakage)
- Check motor amperage against nameplate rating

Weekly Checks:
- Inspect mechanical seal for leakage (max 1 drop per minute for packed glands)
- Check alignment between pump and motor using dial indicators
- Verify coupling condition and guard integrity
- Inspect foundation bolts for tightness
- Check lubrication levels in bearing housing

Monthly Checks:
- Perform vibration analysis (overall and spectral)
- Check bearing oil condition or grease levels
- Inspect suction strainer/filter condition
- Verify instrument calibration (pressure, temperature, flow)
- Record and trend all readings

Quarterly Checks:
- Full vibration spectrum analysis with trending
- Oil analysis for bearing housings (check for metal particles, water contamination)
- Thermographic inspection of motor, bearings, and couplings
- Check impeller clearance (back pull-out design)
- Inspect wear rings for excessive clearance

Annual Overhaul:
- Complete disassembly and inspection
- Replace mechanical seal
- Check impeller for erosion, corrosion, and cavitation damage
- Measure and record wear ring clearances (replace if > 2x design clearance)
- Re-align pump and motor
- Replace bearings if required
- Hydrostatic test casing if applicable

3. COMMON FAILURE MODES AND TROUBLESHOOTING
--------------------------------------------

Problem: Excessive Vibration
Causes:
- Misalignment between pump and motor (most common cause)
- Unbalanced impeller (erosion, debris buildup)
- Bearing wear or failure
- Cavitation (NPSH issue)
- Loose foundation bolts
- Pipe strain on pump casing
Diagnosis: Perform spectral vibration analysis. 1x RPM indicates imbalance; 2x RPM indicates misalignment; high-frequency indicates bearing defects.
Solution: Laser align pump and motor, balance impeller, replace bearings, verify NPSH available > NPSH required.

Problem: Low Flow / Low Pressure
Causes:
- Impeller wear or damage
- Worn wear rings (excessive internal recirculation)
- Air entrainment in suction line
- Partially closed discharge valve
- Wrong impeller size or speed
Diagnosis: Compare actual performance curve to design curve. Check suction pressure for adequate NPSH.
Solution: Replace impeller or wear rings, check for air leaks in suction piping, verify valve positions.

Problem: Mechanical Seal Failure
Causes:
- Running dry (no liquid at seal faces)
- Excessive vibration or shaft deflection
- Incorrect seal installation
- Chemical attack on seal materials
- Thermal shock
Diagnosis: Check for leakage pattern, inspect seal faces for scoring or heat checking.
Solution: Ensure proper seal flush plan, reduce vibration, verify seal material compatibility with process fluid.

Problem: Bearing Failure
Causes:
- Over-greasing or under-greasing
- Contaminated lubricant (water, dirt)
- Misalignment causing excessive axial or radial loads
- Incorrect bearing fit (too tight or too loose)
Diagnosis: Vibration analysis showing bearing defect frequencies (BPFO, BPFI, BSF, FTF). Elevated bearing temperature.
Solution: Follow manufacturer lubrication specifications, maintain proper alignment, replace bearings with correct fit.

Problem: Cavitation
Causes:
- Insufficient NPSH available
- Suction line restrictions (clogged strainer, partially closed valve)
- Liquid temperature too high
- Excessive suction lift
Diagnosis: Characteristic crackling/gravel noise. Pitting damage on impeller inlet vanes. Fluctuating discharge pressure.
Solution: Increase suction pressure, reduce suction losses, lower liquid temperature, install larger suction piping.

4. SAFETY PROCEDURES
---------------------
- Always perform Lockout/Tagout (LOTO) before any maintenance work
- Drain and flush pump before opening for toxic or hazardous fluids
- Wear appropriate PPE: safety glasses, gloves, steel-toe boots
- Verify zero energy state before removing guards or couplings
- Follow confined space entry procedures if entering pump pits
- Use proper lifting equipment for heavy components (impellers, casings)

5. SPARE PARTS RECOMMENDATIONS
-------------------------------
Critical spares to keep on hand:
- Complete mechanical seal assembly
- Set of bearings (radial and thrust)
- Impeller (spare or refurbished)
- Wear ring set (casing and impeller)
- Coupling element (elastomeric insert or disc pack)
- Shaft sleeve
- Gasket set (casing, bearing housing)
- O-ring kit for seal chamber
